1. THE IVY LEAGUE GROOMING: YALE, SKULL & BONES (1917)

Prescott Sheldon Bush entered Yale University in 1913, inheriting the social standing established by his father’s rail and steel connections. At Yale, Bush was tapped for the secretive senior society Skull and Bones (Class of 1917), an institution whose membership formed an interlocking directorate across the American diplomatic corps, Wall Street investment houses, and the emerging intelligence establishment.

Among his cohort and close campus circle were individuals who would later direct American military and geopolitical power: E. Roland Harriman (younger brother of W. Averell Harriman), Robert A. Lovett (later Secretary of Defense and a key architect of the Central Intelligence Agency), and prominent members of the Whitney, Bundy, and Rockefeller circles.

2. THE WALKER-HARRIMAN NEXUS: G.H. WALKER & CO.

In August 1921, Prescott Bush married Dorothy Wear Walker, daughter of George Herbert ("Bert") Walker. Walker was a wealthy St. Louis investment banker who had moved to Wall Street to establish G.H. Walker & Co.

Walker was known for his sharp financial instincts, acting as the primary private financier and operational partner for W. Averell Harriman, the playboy son of Union Pacific Railroad tycoon E.H. Harriman. Harriman inherited an enormous fortune and aimed to construct an international banking network operating outside the oversight of traditional commercial banks.

3. BROWN BROTHERS HARRIMAN: 59 WALL STREET

In the wake of the 1929 stock market crash, the American banking sector underwent consolidation. On January 1, 1931, W.A. Harriman & Co. merged with the historic British-American private merchant bank Brown Brothers & Company, establishing Brown Brothers Harriman & Co. (BBH) at 59 Wall Street.

BBH became the largest private, unincorporated partnership bank in the United States. Unlike publicly traded commercial banks governed by the Glass-Steagall Act, private merchant partnerships operated with high discretion, shielded from public disclosure requirements and regulatory scrutiny.

The Legal Hand of Sullivan & Cromwell

The complex international holding companies, foreign stock trusts, and cross-border shell subsidiaries used by Brown Brothers Harriman were designed by their legal counsel: Sullivan & Cromwell, led by senior partners John Foster Dulles and his brother Allen Welsh Dulles.

The Dulles brothers specialized in designing corporate structures that masked German beneficial ownership within American and Dutch front companies, creating a legal firewall between Wall Street capital and European industrial syndicates.

4. FRITZ THYSSEN & THE GERMAN STEEL TRUST: VEREINIGTE STAHLWERKE

The principal foreign client that enriched the Harriman-Bush-Walker network was the German steel magnate Fritz Thyssen, head of the industrial conglomerate Vereinigte Stahlwerke (United Steelworks). Established in 1926, the trust controlled over 75% of Germany’s domestic iron ore reserves and produced over 40% of the country’s raw steel, 45% of its structural plates, and a vast share of its coal and military forgings.

5. UNION BANKING CORPORATION: 39 BROADWAY

The Union Banking Corporation (UBC) was incorporated under New York State banking laws in 1924, listing its principal place of business at 39 Broadway—the headquarters of W.A. Harriman & Co. (and later Brown Brothers Harriman).

UBC was not a traditional consumer retail bank; it operated as a closed clearinghouse and corporate trust bank for the Thyssen family empire and their affiliated shipping, coal, and steel cartels.

The Interlocking Operations at 39 Broadway

The corporate interlock was tight: Prescott Bush managed the day-to-day operations of the bank, approved lines of credit, and coordinated the clearance of raw material shipments, high-grade steel scrap, and industrial coal between Thyssen’s holdings in occupied Europe and international commodity markets.

Even after the German invasion of Poland in September 1939 triggered World War II in Europe, UBC continued to operate without interruption, processing millions of dollars in financial transactions for the German industrial cartel through its Manhattan accounts.

6. SILESIAN-AMERICAN CORP & THE POLISH CORRIDOR

Beyond the Union Banking Corporation, Prescott Bush held operational responsibilities as a director of the Silesian-American Corporation, alongside his father-in-law George Herbert Walker.

Incorporated in Delaware in 1926 by W.A. Harriman & Co., Silesian-American held controlling equity in Consolidated Silesian Steel Corporation, a massive coal-mining and zinc-processing conglomerate located in the rich mineral basin of Upper Silesia, along the German-Polish border.

The Proximity to Oświęcim (Auschwitz)

The industrial assets controlled by Silesian-American Corporation were situated in the direct vicinity of Oświęcim (later renamed Auschwitz by the German occupation). Prescott Bush remained on the corporate board of directors of Silesian-American throughout this period, until the company's German-linked assets were seized by the United States government.

7. THE 1942 FEDERAL SEIZURES: TRADING WITH THE ENEMY ACT

On December 7, 1941, the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor brought the United States into formal hostilities with the Axis powers. Germany declared war on the United States four days later, on December 11, 1941. Under federal wartime legislation, all commercial, financial, and trade relations with enemy nations and their corporate fronts were declared illegal under the Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917 (50 U.S.C. App.).

Despite the state of war, the Union Banking Corporation remained open at 39 Broadway, holding Axis assets, managing corporate trusts, and functioning as an American financial bridge for Thyssen-controlled entities.

Office of Alien Property Custodian: Vesting Order No. 248 Signed: Leo T. Crowley • Executed: October 20, 1942 • 7 Fed. Reg. 8597

"Under the authority of the Trading with the Enemy Act, as amended... all right, title, and interest of Bank voor Handel en Scheepvaart, N.V., in and to 4,000 shares of common capital stock of Union Banking Corporation, a New York corporation... is hereby vested in the Alien Property Custodian of the United States."

Official Finding: "Finding that the said Union Banking Corporation is owned or controlled by or held for the benefit of... nationals of a designated enemy country (Germany)."

Action: The federal government formally seized the bank, its vaults, its assets, and its stock certificates. Prescott Bush was stripped of his directorship by executive decree.

The Subsequent Seizure Orders

The federal crackdown did not stop with Union Banking Corporation. In the weeks that followed, the Alien Property Custodian issued additional vesting orders targeting the surrounding network:

  • Vesting Order No. 259 (October 1942): Seized Holland-American Trading Corporation (a front firm managed through 39 Broadway).
  • Vesting Order No. 261 (October 1942): Seized Seamless Steel Equipment Corporation (a direct Thyssen front).
  • Vesting Order No. 370 (November 1942): Seized the Nazi-controlled stock in Silesian-American Corporation, terminating Prescott Bush's management role over the Silesian mining properties.

8. POST-WAR LIQUIDATION: DRESSER & TEXAS PETROLEUM

The seizure of Union Banking Corporation's shares did not result in the permanent forfeiture of the underlying capital. Following the conclusion of World War II, the Alien Property Custodian held the assets in federal escrow pending legal and financial settlement.

In 1951, the federal government liquidated the remaining assets of the Union Banking Corporation. As a shareholder of record, Prescott Bush received a cash distribution from the liquidation of approximately $1,500,000 (equivalent to over $18 million today)—a windfall derived from an enterprise that had been seized for servicing enemy industrial cartels.

The Launch of George H.W. Bush

This financial windfall, coupled with Brown Brothers Harriman credit lines, directly capitalized his son, George Herbert Walker Bush. Rather than following his father onto the trading floor of Wall Street, G.H.W. These offshore drilling assets in the Caribbean and the Gulf of Mexico provided logistical infrastructure, maritime cover, and financial foundations that intersected with early American intelligence operations in the region, paving the way for G.H.W. Bush’s subsequent rise to Director of Central Intelligence (CIA) in 1976.

9. THE SENATE YEARS: EISENHOWER & THE SECURITY STATE

With his family fortune secured, Prescott Bush transitioned into direct political office. After a narrow defeat in 1950, he was elected to the United States Senate from Connecticut in 1952, serving until January 1963.

In Washington, Bush was an establishment insider, playing regular rounds of golf with President Dwight D. Eisenhower and serving as an influential voice on the Senate Banking and Currency Committee and the Joint Economic Committee.
